Robotics Club — Sponsorship Portfolio Website
Complete Design Brief + Ready-to-Use AI Build Prompt

Purpose of this document: This is a single-page sponsor-facing portfolio website brief. The primary goal is not "showing off the club" — it's convincing a sponsor to give money/support for the upcoming event. Every section below is designed with that one goal in mind. Everything else (committee, past events, workshops) exists to build trust and credibility so the sponsorship ask at the end feels safe and worthwhile.

1. WHAT REAL UNIVERSITY ROBOTICS CLUBS DO (and why we're not copying them directly)

Research into MIT RoboTeam, MIT Robocon, Combat Robotics Club, etc. shows their official sites are actually very plain — mostly text, no flashy design, because they're built for internal recruitment, not fundraising.

For a sponsorship portfolio, the better reference point is tech hackathon / startup landing pages — dark theme, bold typography, clear numbers, and a dedicated "Partner With Us" section. That's the direction this brief takes.

Rule of thumb: Keep neon accents to under ~20% of the visible layout — use them for CTAs, hover states, and key numbers only. Everything else stays dark and neutral so it reads as professional, not like a gaming site. Avoid pure white text on the dark background — it causes glare; use the ice-white shade above.

3. SITE STRUCTURE (single page, in this exact order)
Hero — Club name, university name, logo, tagline, nav
About the Club — one short paragraph, mission
Leadership / Committee — Convener, Co-Convener, core team
Flagship Events — Robo Expo 01, 02, 03 (with growth story)
Workshops — grid of workshops conducted
What's Next — the mysterious future-event teaser
Partner With Us — THE sponsorship section (most important for your goal)
Footer — contact, socials, university affiliation
4. SECTION-BY-SECTION CONTENT BLUEPRINT
1) Hero Section
University logo + Club logo side by side (or lockup), small "Est. [YEAR]" tag
Big headline: [CLUB NAME] in Space Grotesk Bold
Subheading: [UNIVERSITY NAME] + one-line tagline (e.g. "Building the future, one robot at a time")
Background: dark with subtle animated circuit lines or particle dots
CTA buttons: "Meet the Team" (scroll) + "Partner With Us" (scroll to sponsorship, amber button — this should be visible from the very first screen)
2) About the Club
2–3 sentences: what the club does, since when, what makes it different
3–4 quick stat chips here if you have them: e.g. 150+ Members, 3 Robo Expos, X Workshops, X Sponsors Partnered — numbers build instant credibility for sponsors scrolling fast
3) Committee
Convener + Co-Convener featured larger (bigger photo card, glowing border)
Rest of the committee in a clean grid below
Each card: circular photo, name, role (in Orbitron micro-label style), 1-line responsibility, optional LinkedIn icon
Photos should be consistent (same background/crop) — inconsistency looks unprofessional to sponsors
4) Flagship Events — Robo Expo 01 / 02 / 03
Present as a progression/timeline, not just 3 separate boxes — this tells a growth story sponsors love
Each Expo card includes: name/edition badge (EXPO — 01 style label), date, 1–2 line description, and numbers: participants, teams, footfall, prize pool if any, media coverage if any
If Expo 03 is your biggest yet, visually make its card the largest/most prominent — this proves momentum and justifies "sponsor the next one"
5) Workshops
Simple grid of cards: workshop title, date, one-line description, small icon
If there are many, show top 6–8 and add a "+X more" chip rather than listing everything
6) What's Next — Mystery Teaser
This section should look and feel different from the rest — darker, more atmospheric, minimal text
Use a partially obscured/glitch-style graphic or silhouette (a blurred robot shape, redacted text blocks) rather than full text
Copy should be short and cryptic: one line like "Something is being built." or "[REDACTED] — Coming Soon"
Optional countdown timer if a date is set
CTA here should tie back to sponsorship: "Be part of it before anyone else — Partner With Us" — this turns curiosity directly into your sponsorship pitch
7) Partner With Us (Sponsorship — the most important section)

This is where the money decision gets made. Structure it like this:

Headline that speaks to the sponsor's benefit, not yours: e.g. "Power the Next Robo Expo" (not "We need money")
Why sponsor us — 3 short benefit points (brand visibility to X engineering students, media/social reach, association with innovation)
Sponsorship tiers table (even a simple 3-tier version works): Title / Gold / Silver — with a couple of benefit rows compared side by side
Past sponsors logo wall (if you have any past sponsors — even small ones — showing this builds instant trust)
Clear CTA: a prominent amber button — "Become a Sponsor" linking to email/contact, plus a "Download Sponsorship Deck (PDF)" option if you have one
Direct contact info: email + phone, so a sponsor doesn't have to hunt
8) Footer
University + club affiliation line
Social media icons
Contact email
Small print / copyright
5. SPONSOR-CONVERSION CHECKLIST (don't skip these)
 Real numbers everywhere possible (attendees, teams, reach) — sponsors respond to numbers, not adjectives
 "Partner With Us" CTA visible in the hero AND repeated at the end — don't make sponsors scroll to find it
 Past sponsor logos shown if you have any, even one
 Growth story across Expo 01→02→03 clearly visible (this is your strongest persuasion tool)
 Direct, easy contact method — a sponsor should never have to search for how to reach you
 Mobile-friendly — many people will open this link on phone first
 Fast loading — no heavy unoptimized images/videos
6. TECHNICAL / UX NOTES FOR THE BUILDER
Single-page, smooth-scroll navigation with a sticky top nav (logo + jump links + "Partner With Us" button always visible)
Scroll-triggered fade-in/slide-up animations on each section — subtle, not overdone
Hover glow effects on cards (cyan border glow) for that tech feel
Fully responsive (mobile-first) — sponsors often open links from email/WhatsApp on phone
Keep contrast high enough for readability (ice-white text on near-black background passes accessibility comfortably)
Optimize images before upload (compressed logos/photos) so the page loads fast
